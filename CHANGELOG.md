# Changelog

## v1.57.0 - peg holes drawn

- **Peg holes on the drawing.** A board with pegs glued into it now shows the holes on its detail drawing, with their size, depth and positions measured from the end, ready to mark out.

## v1.56.0 - risers

- **Step stools and stairs:** the upright board under the front of a step is now named a riser. The guide no longer says to "centre" a tread on the height of its stepped side.

## v1.55.0 - pegs and wall shelves

- **Coat racks and peg rails.** Dowel pegs set into a board are now glued into drilled holes. The board's drawing lists the hole size, depth and centres. Before, they were reported as two parts in the same space.
- **Wall shelves:** a piece that hangs on the wall, like a back board with brackets and a shelf, now gets a "Hang it on the wall" note on screwing into studs, instead of a tip-over warning. Its parts are named back board, bracket and shelf.
- **Slats** are spaced evenly with a spacer block even when they're cut the same as other boards in the piece.

## v1.54.0 - planters

- **Planters and boarded walls.** Boards stacked on edge into a wall, like the sides of a planter or crate, are now side boards and end boards. They are screwed to the legs; before, the guide told you to edge-glue them to each other. The ends are built as frames first.
- **Cap rails** are screwed straight down instead of fixed with brackets from below.
- **Cleats** are screwed on from their own side, so the screw heads stay inside, for example along a bed's side rails.
- **"Set one flush with the top, the other 5-1/2 in down"** now also covers a board stacked on top of another.

## v1.53.0 - grain on show faces

- **Grain runs the right way on doors and drawer fronts.** In the cutting plan, doors, door panels, stiles and rails, drawer fronts, lids, headboards and footboards now keep their grain running along their length by default, the way a cabinetmaker would cut them. Sometimes this uses a little more sheet. You can untick "grain" on any item in the cut list to let the plan turn it.

## v1.52.0 - easels

- **Easels and A-frames.** On an easel, the back leg is now hinged on last with a pair of butt hinges, with a chain or cord to the front legs so they can't spread. The board the canvas stands on is named the ledge.
- **Parts that meet along a line** no longer show as floating. A board across leaning legs, for example, touches them only along one edge; these are now found and joined.

## v1.51.0 - plinths and crates

- **Gallery plinths and art crates.** A display plinth's front used to be mistaken for a door, and so did a crate's screwed-on lid. They are now plain sides. Strips screwed across the face of a panel, like a crate's battens, are named battens instead of face-frame stiles.
- For a tall piece with no doors or drawers, such as a plinth, the tipping check now also says how to keep it steady away from a wall: weigh down the base with sandbags or bricks on a low shelf.

## v1.50.0 - tapered legs

- **Tapered legs.** A leg that tapers on some of its faces used to be listed as a solid block to glue up from layers. It is now a plain board in the cut list, with the taper described: which faces, the foot size and where the taper starts. It gets its own drawing and the steps to make it: cut the joinery while the leg is still square, then use a taper jig on the table saw, or the band saw, and finish with a plane.
- Tapered legs and raised panels now appear in the tools check, so the app can say if your tools can't make them.

## v1.49.0 - bed centre support, long side front

- **Wide beds:** when a bed's slats span more than 40 in with nothing under the middle, the check now asks for a centre rail with a leg to the floor, as full-size and larger beds need. Before, it only suggested thicker slats.
- **Which way it faces:** a table, bench or chest drawn end-on, with no doors or drawers to show its front, is now turned so its long side faces you.

## v1.48.0 - panelled bed ends

- **Panelled headboards and footboards.** In a bed whose ends have a panel between the posts, the guide now says "make the headboard" and "make the footboard". The posts stay legs and the side rails hang on bed rail fasteners. Before, the posts were treated as thin door stiles.
- Front and back stretchers that differ: the guide now says "set one flush with the front edge, and the other 1/4 in from the back edge", instead of only the second.

## v1.47.0 - raised panels

- **Raised-panel doors.** A panel with bevelled edges, the classic raised panel, is now recognised. The cut list names it with its edge thickness and bevel width. It gets its own drawing and the steps to raise it with a router table or a tilted saw blade. The grooves in the frame are sized to its thin edge.
- Long part descriptions no longer run off the edge of the drawing sheet or the guide page.

## v1.46.0 - five-piece drawer fronts

- **Five-piece drawer fronts.** A frame-and-panel drawer front is now made on the bench as one piece ("make the drawer front"). It is screwed on from inside once the drawer hangs, with a single instruction. Its parts are named drawer front stile, rail and panel.
- **Inset Shaker doors in a face frame** get inset face-frame hinges on both doors. Before, one door of the pair could be given cup hinges.
- **Clearer door pictures.** The bench step for a frame-and-panel door, drawer front or panelled frame now shows the panel in the middle, with the stiles pulled out to the sides and the rails above and below.
- Frames with short rails (narrow doors) are now recognised.

## v1.45.0 - frame-and-panel doors

- **Frame-and-panel (Shaker) doors and panelled sides.** A thin panel sitting in grooves in the stiles and rails round it is now understood. The app no longer reports the groove overlap as two parts in the same space. The strips' drawings show the grooves, and the guide gives the groove size.
- Frame-and-panel doors are made on the bench ("make 2 doors"): lay a stile down, fit the rails, slide the panel in, close up with the other stile. They are hung on hinges at the end and never screwed to the case. The stile drawing says which stile takes the hinge cups.
- Panelled fronts and backs (a blanket chest's, say) are made on the bench too, with muntins named as such. The case is pocket-screwed to them from inside, so no screw shows outside. A lid on a panelled chest gets its piano hinge along the back frame's full width.
- The guide says a solid-wood panel must not be glued in, so it can move with the seasons; a plywood panel can be.

## v1.44.0 - Outdoor timber

- Outdoor projects: pressure-treated pine, cedar, redwood and teak are in the material list. With any of them, the shopping list says to use exterior-rated screws and waterproof glue.

## v1.43.0 - Bevel drawings

- Bevelled strips (French cleats) get their own detail drawing, with the angle noted.

## v1.42.0 - French cleats

- French cleats: a strip with one edge bevelled is listed as such in the cut list, with how to rip it. A cabinet hung on a French cleat gets a last step that screws the wall half to the studs and hooks the cabinet on.

## v1.41.0 - Guard rail height

- Bunk and loft beds: the check tells you the thickest mattress the guard rails allow, since they must stand 5 in above the mattress.

## v1.40.0 - Spindle gaps

- Cribs and railings: the check warns when the gaps between spindles are wider than is safe for a crib (2-3/8 in) or a stair railing (4 in).

## v1.39.0 - Wording

- Small wording fixes in the hinge and worktop-bolt instructions.

## v1.38.0 - Fit directions

- Fixed: in some tables, workbenches and chairs the rails and aprons were missing the direction to fit them from, and weren't drawn pulled out.

## v1.37.0 - Squared up

- Models drawn at an angle (turned on the floor plan in the 3D program) are squared up on import, so the cut list, drawings and guide come out right. The project notes say when this happened.

## v1.36.0 - Frames are not ladders

- Fixed: a frame of boards (a desk's O-shaped leg, once split into its pieces) was mistaken for a ladder.

## v1.35.0 - L-shaped kitchens

- L-shaped kitchens: both runs of cabinets are recognised and built as whole cabinets.

## v1.34.0 - Platform beds

- Platform beds: the frame goes up first, then the centre beam and ledgers, then all the slats in one step, then the headboard.

## v1.33.0 - Headboards

- Beds: a headboard (and a footboard) between the posts is now called that.

## v1.32.0 - Shorter start

- Guides are a page shorter: the first step no longer just says "place the side". It places it and fits the next parts in the same step.

## v1.31.0 - Facing the front

- A model drawn facing sideways or backwards is now turned to face you on import, so the Front view, the drawings and the guide's "from the front" are right. Which way is up (project menu) has Turn left and Turn right buttons if it needs fixing.
- Telling which way is up is more reliable: a chair is no longer stood on its head, and a cabinet facing sideways is no longer laid on its back.

## v1.30.0 - Frames together

- Benches and tables built from end frames now always start by setting out both frames together, whichever way round the model was drawn.

## v1.29.0 - Consistent fixing

- Two alike strips screwed up into a top are now described the same way, in one line.

## v1.28.0 - Big runs

- Big kitchens: all the doors (up to 24) are hung in one step instead of being split oddly across two.

## v1.27.0 - Splayed legs

- Picnic tables and anything with splayed board legs: the legs are recognised as legs, and each end (two legs and their crosspieces) is made as a frame on the bench.

## v1.26.0 - Chests and lids

- Blanket chests and toy boxes:
  - The plinth is recognised and made first, and the case is built on it.
  - The lid hangs on a piano hinge, with a lid support that holds it open at any angle, both on the shopping list.
  - The check reminds you why the support matters, especially in a toy box.

## v1.25.0 - Drawers on wooden runners

- Tables with a drawer on wooden runners: the runners, kickers and drawer rails are recognised, the runners and kickers go on the side frames on the bench, and the drawer just slides in on waxed runners, with no metal slides on the shopping list.

## v1.24.0 - Ladder rungs

- Ladders: the guide gives the height of the lowest rung and the step between rungs, marked on both sides at once, instead of a list of heights.

## v1.23.0 - Wall cabinets

- Wall cabinets: the strip across the back under the top is recognised as a hanging rail. The shopping list has the screws to hang it, and the guide says how to hang it on the studs.

## v1.22.0 - Small fixes

- Thin shaped panels are no longer mistaken for corbels.
- A project you just saved reads "Just now" on the home screen.

## v1.21.0 - Frame order

- Frames with spindles (crib ends, chair backs) now read in true order: bottom rail, spindles, top rail, then the second leg.

## v1.20.0 - Chairs

- Chairs: the seat is called the seat, and the rails up the back are back rails with the splats between them. A chair's back is no longer mistaken for a ladder.

## v1.19.0 - Clearer bench steps

- Bench steps read in the order you build: lay down one rail, stand the spindles on it, put the other rail on top (and for frames: one leg, the rails, then the other leg).
- Step pictures place "×N" tags more clearly and no longer count parts going in different ways together.

## v1.18.0 - Cribs and spindles

- Cribs and railings: upright slats between two rails are now spindles. Each side is made flat on the bench as a slatted panel, and the guide gives the gap and a spacer block instead of a list of positions.
- Any flat frame (a crib end, a face frame) is made in one bench step, whatever its number of parts.

## v1.17.0 - Islands and corbels

- Kitchen islands: the brackets under a seating overhang are called corbels, the solid back is the back, and the top strips are stretchers.
- A case's build now starts from a side, not from its back.
- Step pictures no longer put "×2" beside two parts that only share a size.

## v1.16.0 - Ladders

- Loft and bunk beds with a ladder: the ladder is recognised (sides and rungs), made flat on the bench in one step, and fitted last, from the side.
- A desk top under a loft bed sits on cleats, now called cleats.
- A guard rail that stops at the ladder opening is called a guard rail.

## v1.15.0 - Bunk beds: guard rails and slats in one go

- Bunk beds: the rails round the top bunk are now called guard rails and hook on like the side rails, so the bed still comes apart.
- All a bed's slats are laid in one step after the cleats, instead of three or four steps of a few slats each.
- Cleats are screwed on from the inside, so no screw heads show on the outside of the rails.

## v1.14.0 - Numbered cuts on the cutting plan

- Cutting plans now number the cuts on each sheet in the order to make them, so you can break a sheet down without working it out at the saw, or ask the store to make the first rips.

## v1.13.0 - Mortise wording

- Clearer wording for mortised legs: "Before you build" mentions mortises, and the second mortise of the same size is a one-line step instead of repeating the whole method.

## v1.12.0 - Mortises on the drawings

- Leg drawings show their mortises: the slots are drawn (hidden lines where you can't see them) and dimensioned, not only described in the notes.

## v1.11.0 - Units everywhere

- The "Making the parts" steps now give every groove, rabbet and mortise size and position with its unit, which matters most in millimetres.
- Sliding-door track is listed as "at least" the opening's length, rounded to whole millimetres.
- Cutting plan: the first page is called "What to buy", so the label is no longer cut off.

## v1.10.0 - Fix stair names on desks

- Fixed: since v0.98, a desk with notched end panels could have its top and shelves called "treads" and its ends "stringers". Only real steps, each board standing further back than the one below, are called that now.

## v1.9.0 - Lettered sub-assemblies

- Complicated builds read more easily: sub-assemblies without a name of their own are lettered A, B, C… ("make sub-assembly B", then "fit sub-assembly B from step 4"), instead of "join items 3, 4, 7, 8, 15 and 16" and "the sub-assembly from step 4".

## v1.8.0 - Models stand up by themselves

- Models saved with Y up (Blender, Fusion and others by default) are now stood up automatically on import, instead of arriving lying on their backs. The project notes say when this happens. If the guess is wrong, use Which way is up in the project menu.
- Mitred boxes: the glue-up step now says to fold the sides up around the bottom.

## v1.7.0 - Clearer 3D view

- 3D view: the Front / Top / Right / 3D buttons now sit in a row along the top, so they no longer cover the end of a wide model.
- Watching the build steps in 3D: each step's title shows in full (up to two lines) instead of being cut off.

## v1.6.0 - Tidier exploded views

- Exploded drawings are much tidier on big pieces: a bookcase's shelves are pulled out together in neat columns instead of a staircase down the page, and a chest's drawers come out side by side.

## v1.5.0 - Cleaner exploded cabinet runs

- Kitchen runs: the exploded drawing shows the cabinets pulled apart along the row, with the worktop above them, instead of scattered front and back.

## v1.4.0 - Leaning parts go in the right way

- Leaning parts: a bench's back slats now go on from the front, in front of their supports, instead of "from the back" through them.

## v1.3.0 - Stronger leg joints

- Workbenches and tables with thick legs: the aprons and rails are pocket-screwed into the legs from their hidden faces, instead of long counterbored screws driven through the legs into the rails' end grain. That's 32 fewer plugs on the workbench template.
- Thin back panels (6 mm) get brads instead of "too small for fasteners".
- Parts that meet at an angle without sharing a flat face still get screw steps.

## v1.2.0 - Sliding doors and L desks

- Sliding doors are recognised. They run in a double track (on the shopping list, cut to the opening) with flush pulls, instead of being screwed to the case as panels.
- L-shaped desks: the two tops are joined with worktop connector bolts from underneath, instead of only being edge-glued. The return's end panel is called a side.

## v1.1.0 - One-step frame glue-ups

- Picture frames (anything that lies flat as a whole) are laid out and glued up in one step, instead of being split over two.
- Tenoned rails read naturally: "Glue the tenons into 2 aprons", then "the 2 aprons from step 2".

## v1.0.0 - Tidier Check tab

- Check tab: the buttons under a finding (More, Show in 3D, the fixes) wrap onto a new line instead of being cut off at the edge, and the filters fit on narrow screens.
- The project header is a little tighter, so more of the overall size shows.
- Version 1.0: just the number after 0.99.

## v0.99.0 - Mortise and tenon

- Mortise and tenon: tenons drawn as parts of their own are recognised. The guide glues each tenon into its rail on the bench, then glues the rails into the legs' mortises. No screws or brackets, and no false "parts overlap" problem. Each leg's drawing and making steps give every mortise's size and position, and how to cut it.
- Step stool treads go on from above onto the stringers.
- Fixed: a wardrobe's hanging rod could be named a back slat.

## v0.98.0 - Stringers and treads

- Step stools and short stairs: notched sides are called stringers and the boards on them treads. Treads are screwed down into the stringers instead of hung on brackets.
- Parts that sit in a notch or step of another part now get proper fixing instructions instead of "then fasten".

## v0.97.0 - Angles on the drawing

- Assembly drawings now say the angle of anything set off upright, like "items 1 and 6 are set 15° off upright, leaning back", or a stool's splayed legs, so you don't have to guess it from the picture.
- A bench's leaned-back boards are named back slats and back supports.

## v0.96.0 - Beds that come apart

- Beds now come apart: side and end rails hang on bed rail fasteners (with how to fit them) instead of being glued and screwed, so a big bed can go through a door. The shopping list counts the pairs.
- Parts set at an angle, like a garden bench's leaned-back slats, now get proper screw instructions.
- Bench seat slats are screwed down, not bracketed from below.
- No more 4 in deep counterbores: past 2 in the guide uses brackets.

## v0.95.0 - Face-frame cabinets

- Face-frame cabinets: the rail between a drawer and the doors is now built into the face frame, inset doors hang on inset face-frame hinges with card shims for even gaps, and drawer slides get spacer strips so they clear the frame (the step says how thick).
- A base cabinet's top strips are called stretchers.

## v0.94.0 - Mitres and hutches

- Mitres: picture frames and mitred boxes are recognised. The cut list says "ENDS CUT AT 45° (MITRE)" or "(BEVEL)", the drawing says which way to set the saw and that length is point to point, and the guide glues the corners properly: a band clamp for flat frames, the tape-and-fold method for boxes, then brads, splines or V-nails. No more "mark the notches with a jigsaw" or paper templates for angled ends (splayed legs fixed too).
- Hutches: a desk with shelves on top builds the hutch as its own unit on the bench (back panel last), then sets it on from above and screws it up through the desk top from underneath. The desk top is called a top, and isn't screwed through its face.
- Trestle tables: steps now say "make 2 trestle ends" and "set out the 2 trestle ends".
- Placing lines no longer repeat themselves for matching shelves beside different uprights.
- Metric counterbore depths are given in whole millimetres.

## v0.93.0 - Round and oval tops, trestles, wardrobes

- Round tops: no more 20 pages of paper template. The drawing gives one "36 DIA" and the steps tell you to swing an 18 in radius with a trammel (or a pencil on a string), then true the edge with a router on a circle jig.
- Oval tops: marked out with two nails and a string (the drawing and steps give where the nails go and how long the string is). Cut lists now say ROUND or OVAL.
- Very thick parts (3 in trestle posts and feet) are counterbored and plugged instead of fixed with corner brackets; the plugs are on the shopping list.
- Trestle tables: each end (foot, post, top rail) is built flat on the bench first, then the two ends are stood up and joined by the stretcher. Parts are now named foot, post, top rail and stretcher.
- Wardrobes: the hanging rod is called a rod, and the guide fits closet rod sockets at the right height instead of screwing it.
- Back panels go on last, all together, once the shelves and top are in (it squares the case). Fixes bookcases with several bays and desks where the back went on too early.
- Groove and rabbet steps on the "Making the parts" page now say where each cut goes.
- Fixed: the hardware list for a step could read "#8 x 1-1/4 in up" instead of screws.

## v0.92.0 - Fewer template pages

- **Fewer pages to print**: full-size paper templates are only made for curved or angled shapes. Square-cut shapes are marked out from their drawing with a rule and square (the sample desk went from 100 template pages to 25).

## v0.91.0 - Clearer hardware lists

- **Clearer hardware lists**: screws read as you'd ask for them: "26 screws, #8 x 1-1/2 in" (or "4 x 40 mm").
- Every template was stress-tested with random sizes and options; no problems found.

## v0.90.0 - Dados on the drawings

- **Dados on the drawings**: a side with dados and rabbets now has them drawn on its detail sheet, each placed by a dimension. The notes say where each groove is and which edge each rabbet runs along.

## v0.89.0 - Dados kept, readable names

- **Dados and rabbets**: if your model lets shelves or a back panel into the sides (the way dado joints are usually drawn), "Fix" now keeps them as housing joints and the guide says to glue them in. Before, it trimmed them back to butt joints. The side's drawing shows the grooves.
- **Readable names**: a project imported from "kitchen_run.stl" is called "Kitchen run".

## v0.88.0 - Dowels, spacing, metric boards

- **Dowels** are suggested only where they work (an end or edge into a face). Slats on cleats and thin backs are screwed or pinned instead.
- **Spacing** instructions skip gaps with something in them, and work bay by bay in bookcases.
- Metric board sizes are given in whole millimetres (19 mm, not 19.1).
- Busy steps get a proper title ("the kick board, the divider and 2 more").

## v0.87.0 - Complex project test on device

- Bookcases with several bays sharing uprights are built as one piece, not as separate "cabinets".
- Every release is now also tested on a phone with a large, complicated project (a four-cabinet kitchen run).

## v0.86.0 - Runs of cabinets

- **Runs of cabinets**: identical cabinets side by side (a kitchen run) are recognised. The guide builds them all together over a few bench steps, then sets them out in a row and screws them to each other (no glue, so they come apart to move), then fits the worktop, doors and drawers.
- **Worktops** are screwed on from below through the stretchers. A worktop longer than a sheet suggests buying a ready-made one.
- Doors in a row of cabinets are found and hung properly.

## v0.85.0 - Angled legs and shaped parts

- **Angled legs**: splayed legs and other sticks with ends sawn off square are listed with their angles ("ends cut at 8° and 8°, compound"), and their detail drawing tells you to set both mitre and bevel.
- **Round tables** on legs set well in are no longer said to have "centre legs".
- **Odd-size legs** (say 2-1/2 in square) are planned from a 4x4 planed or ripped down, instead of "not in the plan".
- Step titles read better: "the bottom and 3 shelves".

## v0.84.0 - Face frames, pocket screws, pedestal desks

- **Face frames** are built properly: stiles and rails are joined flat on the bench with pocket screws on the back (or dowels), then glued and pinned to the front of the finished case, before doors and drawers.
- **Pedestal desks and similar builds** start from the pedestal, and a far end panel goes in with the part that joins it, not propped up on its own from the first step.
- **Pocket screws where they belong**: only for ends and edges meeting a face. Slats on cleats, boards lying on supports and thin backs get ordinary screws or brads.

## v0.83.0 - Tested on complex furniture

Tested against much more complicated furniture: a six-drawer dresser, a kitchen base cabinet, a bunk bed, a pedestal desk and a face-frame bookcase. What that fixed:
- **Face frames** are recognised (stiles and rails), and sides under an overhanging top stay "sides".
- **Drawers**: fronts are screwed through the box end with short screws; a whole chest of drawers is hung in one step; slide positions are measured up from the cabinet bottom, once per row.
- **Doors** go on in their own step, never mixed with the case.
- **Tops** between the sides are screwed through the sides; no brackets into a thin back panel.
- **Bunk beds and other tall builds** go together bottom up, deck by deck, with slat spacing for each deck.
- **Clearer pictures**: long strips no longer fly far off and shrink the drawing.

## v0.82.0 - Solid wood, glued up

- **Solid wood done right**: choose oak, pine or another timber and wide panels are planned as boards glued edge to edge, not as a 4 × 8 sheet of oak (which isn't sold). The cutting plan buys the boards, and "Before you build" explains the glue-up. Solid wood skips edge banding.
- **Tops that can move**: a solid wood top on brackets gets the advice to use slotted brackets or tabletop fasteners, so it doesn't split as the seasons change.
- **Drawer boxes**: 1/2 in sides are glued and pinned with brads instead of being sent to a rabbet.
- The material you pick now reaches every part, so instructions follow it.

## v0.81.0 - Screws as sold, pilot holes, hinge sides

- **Screws as you'd buy them**: gauge and length ("#8 x 1-1/2 in", "4 x 40 mm"), chosen from the thickness of the board they go through.
- **Pilot holes**: "Before you build" gives the drill bit for each screw size, for softwood and plywood or hardwood and MDF.
- **Doors**: a pair of doors hangs from its outer edges, so they open from the middle. A single door says to choose its hinge side. Hinges go about 3 in from the top and bottom.

## v0.80.0 - Where the drawer slides go

- **Drawer slides say where they go**: each hang-the-drawer step gives the level line to draw on the cabinet sides (so far down from the top edge) and where the front of the slide sits. Graduated drawers get all their lines in one instruction, top drawer first.
- Build steps of complex imported models no longer list a dozen small offsets. Positions are given only where they read as a clear instruction.

## v0.79.0 - Where parts go

- **The guide now says where parts go** when nothing else places them, measured from the model:
  - "Set each leg 3/4 in from each end of the side rail"
  - "Set the cleat 1-3/4 in down from the top edge"
  - "Set the stretcher 6 in up from the bottom of the leg"
  - "Set the modesty panel 1-1/2 in from the back edge of the side"
  - "Centre the apron on the width of the leg"
- Shelf spacing now says "the shelves (item 3)" instead of "the item 3 parts".

## v0.78.0 - Glued-up tops and first-step spacing

- **Thick tops are glued up**: a panel thicker than any sheet sold (a 1-1/2 in workbench top) is planned as 2 layers of 3/4 in sheet, each cut 1/4 in over size. The cutting plan, shopping list and "Before you build" tell you how to glue and trim it, or to buy a butcher-block panel instead.
- **Edge banding width**: the shopping line says when an edge is thicker than usual and needs wider banding.
- **First step spacing**: pieces that stand apart at the start (a bed's side frames, a table's end frames, a desk's sides) now come with how far apart they go and what sits centred between them.
- **Pictures from the front**: a desk's guide is no longer drawn from behind. Any picture that still is says "Seen from the back".

## v0.77.0 - Dowels as sold, better first steps

- **Dowels sized as sold**, with diameter and length (3/8 x 1-1/2 in, or 8 x 40 mm), and a "Before you build" note on marking and drilling them with a jig.
- **Glue and brads** joints also check for edges too thin to take a brad (a 1/4 in back): those go in a rabbet or against a cleat. The wording names the fixing you chose.
- **A better first step**: pieces that stand on their own next to the first one (a table's second end frame, a bed's side and centre frames, a desk's two sides) are all set out together. The step says to prop or clamp them until the next step joins them.
- **Fewer, clearer steps**: two of the same part fitted from opposite sides is one step ("from the front and back"). Bed slats go in from above instead of sliding in sideways.
- Sub-assembly steps now say which way each board goes on, matching the picture.
- Long step titles are set smaller instead of running off the page.

## v0.76.0 - No fitting from below the floor

## v0.1.0 - Import, parts and cut list

First version. Open an STL of a whole assembly and see what it is made of.

- **Import an STL** with the Import button, or use "Open with" from your file app.
- **Parts are separated automatically.** If the file stored the same body more than once, the extras are removed and the app tells you how many.
- **Units are guessed and can be changed.** STL files don't record units; tap **Change** on the project screen if the size looks wrong, or to switch between millimetres, decimal inches and fractional inches.
- **Cut list.** Identical parts are grouped and counted, and described as panels, boards, rods or shaped pieces with their stock size. Tap an item to set its material. A "stock to buy" total shows the length needed per board size.
- **Share the cut list** as a CSV file that opens in any spreadsheet app.
- **Rename or leave out a part** by tapping it in the Parts tab.
- **Copy diagnostic report** gives you text to paste if something goes wrong.
